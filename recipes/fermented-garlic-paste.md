# Fermented Garlic Paste

*GLP-1 support · Live L. plantarum · Raw finishing paste*

Homemade garlic paste: organic dry garlic powder cultured with *L. plantarum* at ~2% salinity → a sharp, pickle-like paste you stir **raw** into food after cooking.

## Background

Garlic is already a dense organosulfur food (alliin, allicin, and related compounds). Lactic fermentation with *Lactobacillus plantarum* does two extra jobs: it acidifies the paste so the culture can hold the jar against wild yeasts and molds, and it transforms some of garlic’s harsh, heat-sensitive chemistry into more stable, easier-to-absorb metabolites (often discussed as S-allyl cysteine and related postbiotics). The result is less “raw bite,” more tang, and a living matrix meant for the lower gut — not for a hot pan.

Powder is a practical starting point: high surface area, no peeling, and an easy 2% salt target. Garlic is naturally antimicrobial, so a first batch needs a concentrated *L. plantarum* inoculant. Later batches can be backslopped a few generations, with a little honey or sugar as a fuel bridge, then reset before kitchen yeasts take over.

This is a **salted lactic paste**, packed anaerobic and kept cold after it sours — not garlic-in-oil. Always use a clean spoon.

## Ingredients

### Master ingredient base

| Amount | Ingredient |
| --- | --- |
| 1/2 cup | dry garlic powder (organic, pure; no anti-caking agents) |
| 1/2–3/4 cup | warm filtered water (unchlorinated; just enough for a thick paste) |
| 1 tsp | pink Himalayan salt, ground fine (~5–6 g; about 2% salinity) |

## Recipe

Flowchart tables (Cooking For Engineers style) live on the [HTML page](fermented-garlic-paste.html). Method below mirrors those tables.

### Option A: Starting fresh (from powder)

1. **Whisk the liquids.** Whisk the fine pink Himalayan salt and **1/8 tsp** probiotic powder into **1/2 cup** of warm, filtered water until fully dissolved.
2. **Form the paste.** Pour in the **1/2 cup** dry garlic powder. Stir vigorously. If the mixture is too chalky or dry, add water **1 tablespoon at a time** until it is a smooth, thick paste (up to about **3/4 cup** water total).
3. **Pack and seal.** Spoon into the jar. Press firmly with the back of a spoon to eliminate air pockets. Seal with an anaerobic airlock lid.
4. **Ferment.** Place in a dark cupboard at **70°F–75°F** for **3 full days**.
5. **Refrigerate.** When it smells distinctly sharp, tangy, and pickle-like, move it to the fridge to preserve potency.

`[Dissolve salt + culture] → [Stir to thick paste] → [Pack anaerobic] → [3 days at 70–75°F] → [Fridge]`

### Option B: Sustainable backslopping

1. **Awaken the culture.** Dissolve pink Himalayan salt and **1/2 tsp** raw honey or organic sugar into **1/2 cup** warm filtered water. Whisk in **1 Tbs.** of the old fermented paste until thoroughly combined.
2. **Mix and pack.** Stir in **1/2 cup** fresh dry garlic powder until a thick paste forms (add water a tablespoon at a time if needed). Pack tightly, pressing out air bubbles.
3. **Ferment (extended).** Seal the jar. Lower starting bacterial density means **3 to 4 full days** on the counter until the aroma is robust and sour.
4. **Refrigerate.** Transfer to cold storage.

## Best practices

- **Never cook the paste.** Do not add it to boiling food or a hot pan. Stir it **raw** into foods *after* they come off the heat, so live bacteria, sensitive postbiotics, and garlic’s more labile compounds stay intact.
- **Maintain the 4-batch rule.** Do not backslop past 4 or 5 generations. Wild kitchen yeasts will eventually alter the batch. Reset with fresh probiotic powder (Option A) every 4th batch for high-density *L. plantarum*.
- **Keep it strictly anaerobic.** Pack tightly and use an airlock lid. Oxygen favors wild molds and disrupts a clean lactic ferment.
- **Storage lifespan.** Refrigerated, a finished batch stays stable, acidic, and metabolically active for **2 to 3 months** if you always scoop with a perfectly clean spoon.

## Functional Benefits

### Live lactic culture
- **Probiotic:** *L. plantarum* is acid-tolerant and commonly used in vegetable ferments; a dense start helps it outcompete garlic’s own antimicrobials
- **Postbiotic acid:** lactic acid lowers pH, preserves the paste, and is the intended signal environment for a clean ferment
- **Prebiotic leftovers:** a small honey/sugar bridge in Option B is fuel for the culture, not a sweet condiment

### Garlic organosulfurs, fermented
- Dry garlic still carries the alliin / allicin family; lactic fermentation and time in an acidic paste shift some of that chemistry toward more stable, water-soluble metabolites (including S-allyl cysteine as discussed in fermented-garlic research)
- Eating the paste **raw and cold-finished** avoids destroying heat-sensitive sulfur compounds and the live culture at once

### Gut–metabolic framing (GLP-1)
- The recipe is built as an **anaerobic lactic paste** meant to reach the lower gut rather than as a cooked seasoning
- Some garlic fractions and *L. plantarum* metabolites are studied in connection with incretin pathways (including DPP-4–related discussion in the functional-food literature). Treat that as a research direction, not a medical claim or a substitute for prescribed GLP-1 medicines

### Culinary use
- Sharp, pickle-like paste for beans, rice, dressings, and other dishes after they leave the heat
- Small spoonfuls go a long way; salinity is part of the preservation, so taste before adding extra salt to the plate
