# Sprouted Red Beans & Brown Rice

*GLP-1 support · Resistant starch · 24 h cold crash*

This recipe is originally from Dan at [Soulful Kitchen](https://www.instagram.com/soulfulkitchengv/), with some modifications I made at home. Sprouted long-grain brown rice and sprouted small red beans, cooked separately with ghee or coconut oil, then chilled 24 hours.

## Background

Two jars, two pots. The beans take longer than the rice, so start the beans first, and only soak the rice about a day before you expect tails. Cooking with fat, then a full day at fridge temperature, is the point of the protocol: retrograded starch that survives the small intestine and can be fermented in the colon.

Harvest windows follow the sprouts rather than a single 48-hour clock for both.

## Recipe

Flowchart tables (Cooking For Engineers style) live on the [HTML page](sprouted-red-beans-and-brown-rice.html). Method below mirrors those tables.

### Stagger the two jars

Beans are a 2–4 day job. Rice is often ready in 16–24 hours. Start rice only when the beans are about a day from harvest.

| When | Beans | Rice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Soak 8–12 h, or overnight; water 3 in above | — |
| After the soak | Drain; mesh; invert 45°. Rinse every 8 hours | — |
| ~Day 2 | Keep rinsing until tails show | Soak 12 hours (about a day before beans are ready) |
| After rice soak | — | Drain; mesh; invert 45°. Rinse every 8 hours |
| Harvest | 48–96 h total; white tails 0.25–0.5 in | 16–24 h total (up to 48 h RT); sprouts 0.5–1 mm |
| Same evening | Cook, pack, cold crash | Cook, pack, cold crash |

Room temperature sets the clock. Cooler kitchens sit toward 4 days for beans and toward 48 hours for rice. Discard a jar that smells off or turns slimy.

### Track A: Sprouted brown rice

1. **Soak.** Submerge **1 cup** dry long-grain brown rice in filtered water 3 inches above the grain for **12 hours**.
2. **Moist sprout.** Drain completely. Mesh lid; invert the jar at 45°.
3. **Rinse.** Every **8 hours**, fill, swirl, drain fully.
4. **Harvest.** Total soak + sprout **16–24 hours** (up to **48 hours** at room temperature) until 0.5–1 mm sprouts just begin to emerge.

`[Soak 12 h] → [Moist sprout] → [Rinse every 8 h] → [0.5–1 mm sprouts]`

### Track B: Sprouted small red beans

1. **Soak.** Submerge **1 cup** dry small red beans in filtered water 3 inches above for **8 to 12 hours**, or overnight.
2. **Moist sprout.** Drain completely. Mesh lid; invert 45°.
3. **Rinse.** Every **8 hours**, fill, swirl, drain fully.
4. **Harvest.** Total **48–96 hours**. Look for tiny white tails about **0.25–0.5 inches**, usually 2 to 4 days depending on ambient temperature.

`[Soak 8–12 h, or overnight] → [Moist sprout] → [Rinse every 8 h] → [0.25–0.5 in tails]`

### Pot 1: Fluffy rice

1. Add the sprouted rice, **1.25 cups** filtered water, and **1 Tbsp** ghee or coconut oil to a small pot.
2. Bring to a boil **uncovered for 2 minutes**.
3. Cover tightly, drop to the stove’s lowest setting, simmer **15 minutes**. Do not lift the lid.
4. Turn off the heat; steam-rest **covered 10 minutes**.

### Pot 2: Creamy beans

1. Add the sprouted beans, **2 cups** filtered water, **1 Tbsp** ghee or coconut oil, and the bay leaf to a second pot.
2. Rapid rolling boil **uncovered 5 minutes** (lectin step). Sprouting does not replace cooking — do not eat the beans raw.
3. Cover tightly, simmer **15–20 minutes** until the interiors are buttery soft.
4. Turn off the heat. Stir in **1 tsp** sea salt. Smash **2 Tbsp** of beans against the side of the pot for a velvety gravy.

### 24-hour cold crash

1. **Pack within 1 hour** of cooking. Transfer rice and beans into **separate** wide, shallow glass containers (under 3 inches deep).
2. When the heavy steam clears, seal airtight. Refrigerator at **4°C (39°F) for 24 hours**.
3. Next day, scoop rice and beans to taste.
4. Reheat gently on the stovetop with a small splash of water until comfortably warm — do not bring back to a scorching boil.

`[Pack within 1 h] → [Seal when steam clears] → [24 h at 4°C] → [Gentle reheat]`

After the crash, leftover portions freeze. Thaw in the fridge and reheat the same gentle way.

## Best practices

- **Stagger the jars.** Beans 48–96 hours; rice 16–24 hours (up to 48). Start rice when the beans are about a day from tails.
- **Drain completely.** After every rinse, invert the jars at 45° so they stay moist, not sitting in water.
- **Cook the beans hard at the start.** A 5-minute uncovered rolling boil before the simmer is the lectin step.
- **Pack while still hot, not hours later.** Wide containers under 3 inches deep, into the fridge within an hour, lids on once the heavy steam is gone.
- **Reheat, don’t recook.** A scorching boil undoes some of the resistant starch you just waited a day for.

## Functional Benefits

### Resistant starch, butyrate, GLP-1 framing
- **Fat in the pot:** ghee or coconut oil cooked with the starch can form amylose–lipid complexes (often discussed as RS5)
- **Cold crash:** 24 hours at 4°C lets gelatinized starch retrograde into resistant starch type 3 (RS3)
- RS3 is poorly digested up top; colonic microbes (including *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*) ferment it to short-chain fatty acids, chiefly butyrate
- Butyrate can bind FFAR2/3 on colonic L-cells and is studied as a trigger for GLP-1 release. That is a research direction, not a medical claim or a substitute for prescribed GLP-1 medicines

### GABA from sprouted brown rice
- Soaking and sprouting brown rice wakes glutamate decarboxylase in the grain, which converts glutamate toward **GABA**
- Germinated “GABA rice” is associated with a large increase versus unsprouted brown rice; harvest when 0.5–1 mm sprouts just show
- GABA is reasonably heat-stable at a stovetop simmer (the usual figure is stability up to about 105°C). The gentle cook is the point — not a hard rolling boil of the rice
- Dietary GABA and calm/sleep claims are mixed in humans; treat this as a grain-level change, not a neurotransmitter dose

### Anti-nutrients, reduced not erased
- **Phytate:** sprouting turns on phytase, which lowers phytic acid and can free minerals (zinc, magnesium, iron) for absorption
- **Lectins:** the 5-minute rolling boil plus a full simmer is still required for small red beans
- **Oxalates:** soak, sprout, and cook all move the baseline down. They do not take it to zero

### How to eat it
- After the crash, reheat until comfortably warm and portion rice and beans to taste
- Stir [fermented garlic paste](fermented-garlic-paste.html) in after the pan comes off the heat if you want a raw lactic finish
